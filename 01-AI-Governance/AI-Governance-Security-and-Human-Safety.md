# Cybersecurity, AI Security and Human Safety

## The Bigger Question: Are We Building Technology We Can Trust?

Technology has transformed how we communicate, conduct business, manage information and solve complex problems. But as our dependence on software and intelligent systems increases, so does the responsibility to ensure that what we build is secure, reliable and safe.

A software solution can be innovative, beautifully designed and commercially successful, but what happens when its security is compromised?

What happens when the very technology designed to make our lives easier becomes a tool for exploitation?

These are questions that technology professionals, business leaders and Project Managers must begin asking before solutions are deployed.

## 1. Cybersecurity: Can the Software We Build Withstand Attacks?

Cybersecurity is not something to introduce after a product has been developed. It should be considered from the design stage.

Consider a financial application that processes thousands of transactions daily. It may provide excellent user experience, but a vulnerability could expose customer information, compromise accounts or result in financial losses.

This raises important questions:

- How are we protecting user information?
- Have potential vulnerabilities been identified and tested?
- Are access permissions properly managed?
- What happens if the system is compromised?
- Is there an incident response and recovery plan?

Some important practices include threat modelling, vulnerability assessments, penetration testing, encryption, secure authentication, access control and continuous security monitoring.

The objective is not to claim that a system is impossible to attack. It is to build systems that reduce vulnerabilities, detect attacks, limit their impact and recover effectively.

## 2. Learning From Cryptocurrency Hacks

The cryptocurrency ecosystem provides important lessons about digital security.

Incidents involving compromised cryptocurrency exchanges, stolen digital assets and exploited smart contracts demonstrate how a vulnerability in software, infrastructure or operational processes can result in significant financial losses.

Some notable examples include:

- **Mt. Gox (2014):** A major Bitcoin exchange collapse following years of security breaches and the loss of a substantial amount of Bitcoin.
- **Ronin Network (2022):** Attackers compromised validator credentials and stole approximately $625 million in cryptocurrency.
- **Bybit (2025):** A major cryptocurrency theft involving approximately $1.5 billion in digital assets.

These incidents demonstrate that security is not simply about writing good code. It also involves infrastructure protection, credential management, access controls, third-party dependencies, operational security and governance.

A product can be technologically sophisticated and still be vulnerable because one critical control was overlooked.

## 3. AI Security: What Happens When AI Becomes the Attack Surface?

Traditional cybersecurity focuses on protecting software, networks, systems and data.

AI introduces additional security challenges.

For example:

- **Prompt injection:** Malicious instructions manipulate an AI system into behaving in unintended ways.
- **Data poisoning:** Manipulated training data influences a model's behaviour.
- **Sensitive information disclosure:** An AI system exposes information it should not reveal.
- **Model manipulation:** Attackers exploit weaknesses in the model or its surrounding infrastructure.
- **Excessive agency:** An AI agent is given more permissions or autonomy than necessary.

As organisations increasingly integrate AI into business operations, security must extend beyond the application itself to include the models, data, integrations, tools and permissions involved.

## 4. Building AI That Works in Humanity's Interest

Perhaps one of the most important questions in AI development is:

**How do we ensure that the intelligent systems we create do not cause harm to the very people they are designed to serve?**

As AI systems become more capable of making recommendations, executing actions and influencing decisions, we must think carefully about their boundaries.

Some important considerations include:

- Human oversight and meaningful intervention.
- Alignment between system objectives and human intentions.
- Transparency and explainability.
- Fairness and prevention of discriminatory outcomes.
- Clearly defined permissions and limitations.
- Continuous monitoring and accountability.

An AI agent connected to financial systems, for example, should not have unlimited authority to execute transactions without appropriate permissions, controls and approval mechanisms.

The more autonomy we give an AI system, the more important it becomes to define what it can do, what it cannot do and when human intervention is required.

## 5. What This Means for Project Managers

For Project Managers, these considerations should influence how we initiate, plan, execute and monitor technology projects.

We should not only ask whether a solution can be built, how long it will take or how much it will cost.

We should also ask:

1. What could go wrong?
2. What information or assets are we protecting?
3. What security controls are required?
4. Who is accountable when something goes wrong?
5. What level of autonomy should the system have?
6. How will we monitor the solution after deployment?
7. Is there a clear process for responding to incidents?

Security, governance and human safety should be part of the project requirements, acceptance criteria, risk register and ongoing operational planning.

## Final Reflection

The future of technology should not be measured solely by how intelligent, fast or powerful our systems become.

It should also be measured by how responsibly we build them, how securely they operate and how well they protect the people who depend on them.

**The question is no longer just whether we can build it. It is whether we should build it, how safely we can build it and what safeguards must exist before we allow it to operate.**
